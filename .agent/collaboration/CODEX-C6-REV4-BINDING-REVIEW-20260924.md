# C6 Revision 4 — Independent Binding Re-Review

Subject: `factory/c6-rev4` at `54c3ed965cbf034aed2d111fc659f1c3c3398bed`  
Baseline: `6d2a624cfbddd4f668e60eaa8d17dfd1340e711a`  
Review role: independent binding reviewer  
Disposition: **REQUEST_REVISION — not freeze-eligible**

The full SHA-256 values in §1 reproduce at the declared baseline, the four §1.4 NEW paths are absent there, and TEG plus `pre_execute()` are correctly classified as genuinely new design work. Those improvements close the clerical part of rev3 Finding 3. The security and recovery closures are not yet sound, however, and the packet does not reconcile the already-activated mechanism with its `activation_authorization: NONE` state.

## Numbered findings

1. **HIGH — The provider-boundary epoch check and the claimed execution-start CAS are not one atomic linearization.**  
   **Section:** §3.3 steps 1–4; §7 Finding 2.  
   **Exact scenario:** `pre_execute()` reads epoch `E` from the revocation authority. Before the separate gateway lifecycle CAS executes, an authenticated bump durably advances the authority to `E+1`. The gateway then performs its local `held → launch_authorized` CAS using the already-read `E`, mints the token, and reaches `urlopen()`. The bump committed before the packet's declared `launch_authorized` linearization point, yet provider I/O was authorized. A “sole gateway critical section” cannot serialize a different service's epoch commit, and a descriptor-bound token does not repair that ordering. Thus bump-before does not necessarily block I/O and F3 obligation (3) remains open. The design must make epoch comparison and launch authorization one authority-mediated atomic operation, or specify an equivalent shared serialization protocol whose ordering is proven against the bump commit.

2. **HIGH — The Trusted Execution Gateway boundary is internally ambiguous and its required principal separation is outside the authorized inventory.**  
   **Section:** §3.2 principal separation/private surface/adapter bullets; §3.3; §4.  
   **Exact scenario:** §3.2 says the dedicated-principal TEG is the sole context source and its adapter owns the private context, but §3.3 and §4 say caller-side `DirectRunner` calls `pre_execute()` and then performs `urlopen()`. The packet never specifies whether the provider request executes inside the TEG principal, or how an unforgeable single-use launch capability crosses back to the shared owner/agent process without becoming a caller-usable authority carrier. If `DirectRunner` remains caller-side, the privileged context/token boundary is re-exposed; if it moves into the TEG, the provider execution, request/response, output ownership, and cancellation contract is missing from the design and inventory. In addition, baseline Nix explicitly adds `primaryUser` to `aq-lease-signing-clients`, `aq-c2-scheduler-context-clients`, and `aq-revocation-epoch-clients` in three existing service modules. The ALA and C2-SCI modules are verify-only/excluded, so a new `dispatch-gateway.nix` cannot truthfully satisfy the frozen requirement to remove those memberships without an undeclared edit or a broad `mkForce` override of the user's merged groups. The carrier therefore is not yet proven caller-non-suppliable/non-replayable end to end.

3. **HIGH — The re-anchor reproduces file hashes but does not reconcile the live activation state, and P-F4 would invalidate the frozen baseline.**  
   **Section:** §0.1, §1, §3.4, §4, §6.  
   **Exact scenario:** at `6d2a624c`, `nix/modules/services/switchboard.nix` sets `CAPABILITY_SCHEDULER_LEASE_GATE=1`, `nix/modules/profiles/ai-dev.nix` enables the revocation authority, and the service modules grant the shared primary user the private client groups. This is the landed `247a5dd9` activation plus the `4ea5066a` mechanism validation, not a default-OFF/inert tree. None of `switchboard.nix`, `ai-dev.nix`, `lease-signing-authority.nix`, or `c2-scheduler-context-issuer.nix` is bound as an EDIT surface. Consequently the proposed implementation cannot deliver §3.4 default-OFF parity or §3.2 group removal within scope. Separately, §6 requires P-F4 to change the owner allowlist from the normatively hashed revision 3 bytes to revision 4 before operational freeze; doing so necessarily violates §1's base hash and stop-on-drift rule. Rev4 therefore cannot itself be frozen after satisfying its own prerequisite without a fresh re-anchor. The next revision must explicitly model the inherited activation, bind the live wiring files, and order rollback/revocation/provisioning so the freeze baseline remains internally consistent.

4. **HIGH — P-F4 does not yet produce a usable owner bump lever.**  
   **Section:** §2.3 and §6 P-F4.  
   **Exact scenario:** the named `aq-provision-signer-key` can be driven with explicit arguments, but it has no owner-epoch preset; its rotation code marks prior active keys `retired`, not the packet-required `revoked`; and there is no landed Nix/SOPS declaration that materializes `/run/secrets/c6-owner-epoch-bump-key`. More importantly, landed `aq-epoch-bump` deliberately implements a two-step offline-signing workflow and never reads or signs with a SOPS key. An owner can therefore complete the stated provisioner action yet have neither the promised runtime secret nor a specified signer that converts an unsigned bump into a valid request. Manual extraction/signing would reintroduce an undeclared custody path. Add the exact signer/approval boundary, Nix secret declaration and ownership, provisioner behavior, authorized files, and an end-to-end owner bump test; or retain the offline-key model and design its actual custody/signing ceremony instead of claiming the existing SOPS provisioner is sufficient.

5. **HIGH — The intent journal does not deterministically recover every declared W1/W2 path or preserve the two independent replay identities.**  
   **Section:** §2.2 steps 1 and 5; §4.  
   **Exact scenario:** after W1, recovery marks the O_EXCL entry `aborted` and says this releases the idempotency identity, but the same path still exists. The identical retry therefore encounters O_EXCL again; no safe `aborted → intent` reuse/CAS protocol is specified. Conversely, naming the entry only by `sha256(request_id || idempotency_key)` permits the same `request_id` with a different idempotency key, or the same idempotency key with a different request ID, to create a second entry unless a separately specified global uniqueness index/scan exists. After W2, startup recovery is asserted but no startup-before-accept barrier is specified or inventoried: `revocation_epoch_transport.py` is neither an EDIT nor an anchor for such wiring. If another valid bump advances the epoch before the old intent is resolved, later recovery sees an epoch other than both `old_epoch` and `new_epoch` and quarantines instead of reconstructing the committed receipt. The design must freeze recovery-before-listen, independent uniqueness for both replay keys, and an explicit durable aborted-entry retry transition; it must inventory every file needed to enforce those properties.

6. **HIGH — The still-active mechanism-test key/gate requires an explicit disposition before freeze.**  
   **Section:** §0.1 owner-key account; §2.3; §6.  
   **Exact scenario:** `owner-mechtest-2026-08` remains `active` at the baseline while the scheduler gate and epoch authority remain enabled. “Private not retained” is historical narrative, not a verifiable revocation control. Any surviving copy of that throwaway private key can still sign a fleet epoch bump through the primary-user-accessible authority socket, causing unauthorized revocation/denial of scheduled work. This is not a privilege-escalation path, but it is a live integrity/availability authority inconsistent with `activation_authorization: NONE`. P-F4 could revoke it, but P-F4 currently changes the very baseline rev4 freezes and lacks the usable path described in Finding 4. Freeze therefore requires a hash-bound re-scope that first disables the inherited gate and/or revokes the mechanism key, then re-anchors; it cannot defer that live-authority cleanup to a post-freeze footnote.

## Closure assessment

- Rev3 Finding 1: **OPEN** — the dedicated-principal concept is directionally correct, but execution ownership and the actual Nix group-removal surfaces are not closed.
- Rev3 Finding 2: **OPEN** — the epoch reread and gateway CAS retain a bump-between-them race.
- Rev3 Finding 3: **PARTIAL** — listed hashes/absences reproduce, but the live activation inventory and P-F4 ordering contradict the baseline.
- Rev3 Finding 4: **OPEN** — the proposed ceremony does not materialize a callable owner signer/secret path.
- Rev3 Finding 5: **OPEN** — aborted retry, independent replay uniqueness, and recovery-before-accept remain unspecified.

Revision 4 is **not FREEZE-ELIGIBLE**. A corrected, re-anchored revision requires a fresh independent binding review. No implementation, activation, epoch bump, provider traffic, or rollback is authorized by this verdict.

VERDICT: REQUEST_REVISION — atomically couple epoch comparison to launch authorization; make the TEG the unambiguous provider-execution boundary and inventory the real group-removal edits; reconcile and re-anchor the already-live mechanism activation; define a genuinely usable owner signing path; and complete journal replay/recovery semantics
